## [Eddie Tipton: the lottery random number generator that knew three dates a year](https://betkyo.com/en/blog/eddie-tipton-the-lottery-random-number-generator-that-knew-three-dates-a-year/)

_2026-10-29 · 9 min_

Eddie Tipton was the **information security director of the Multi-State Lottery Association (MUSL)**, the body that ran the Hot Lotto draw for Iowa and other states, and he had helped write its random number generator. On **23 December 2010** he bought two Hot Lotto tickets at a Des Moines convenience store; on **29 December 2010** one of them won the **$14.3 million jackpot** (advertised at $16.5 million as an annuity). A New York lawyer tried to claim it for an anonymous Belize trust, **Hexham Investments**, an hour and fifty minutes before the ticket expired on 29 December 2011, and withdrew the claim in **January 2012** rather than say who had bought it. Prosecutors showed Tipton had entered the sealed draw room on **20 November 2010** to adjust the clock for daylight-saving time, that the camera there recorded **about one second per minute** that day, and that a self-deleting program could have been installed from a USB stick. Forensic examination of a sister generator in Wisconsin found extra code that, on **27 May, 23 November and 29 December**, if the date fell on a **Wednesday or Saturday** draw after **8 p.m.**, replaced the random draw with numbers Tipton could compute. Every jackpot tied to him, in **Colorado (2005), Wisconsin (2007), Kansas, Oklahoma (2011) and Iowa (2010)**, fell on 23 November or 29 December. He was convicted in **July 2015**, the conviction was vacated on appeal in June 2017, and that month he **pleaded guilty**, confessed to installing the code around 2005–2006, and was sentenced in **August 2017 to 25 years** with about **$2.2 million** in restitution. He was paroled in **July 2022**. Hot Lotto’s fair odds were **1 in 10,939,383**; for a player who knows the seed they are 1 in 1. The lesson is not that computers cannot be random. It is that a random number nobody outside the room can verify is only a promise, and the man who makes the promise can also keep the ticket.

## [Hamster Kombat: 300 million players, a 60-billion-token airdrop, and the month they left](https://betkyo.com/en/blog/hamster-kombat-300-million-players-a-60-billion-token-airdrop-and-the-month-they-left/)

_2026-10-22 · 8 min_

Hamster Kombat, a Telegram “tap-to-earn” game launched in March 2024, announced **300 million players** on **30 July 2024** and a whitepaper giving **60% of the HMSTR token to players**. The airdrop went live on **26 September 2024**; **131 million** accounts qualified (Decrypt counted about 129 million), **2.3 million** were banned for cheating, and the token fell about **30% in its first 24 hours** (from $0.012 to $0.0087) and to an all-time low of **$0.0025** by 1 November. Monthly players, read from the mini-app’s own counter, went from **over 100 million in late September** to **fewer than 44 million** by early November (ForkLog: **40.9 million**, against a 300 million summer peak; BitPinas: **38 million** on 6 November). In the calculation below, 60 billion tokens spread over 131 million accounts is about **458 tokens each**, roughly **$5 at the listing price** and **$1.15 at the November low**; for a player who tapped ten minutes a day for six months, that is a few cents an hour. On 22 October 2026 CoinGecko lists HMSTR at **$0.000147**, about 98% below its high. A casino game states its edge before the bet and pays by that rule; a tap game pays whatever the issuer decides afterwards, and the player count tells you what people concluded when they found out.

## [Don Johnson: $15 million from Atlantic City, and the 20% rebate that flipped the edge](https://betkyo.com/en/blog/don-johnson-15-million-from-atlantic-city-and-the-20-percent-rebate-that-flipped-the-edge/)

_2026-10-21 · 9 min_

Between **December 2010 and April 2011** Don Johnson, a Bensalem, Pennsylvania racetrack executive and former jockey, won about **$15 million** at three Atlantic City casinos: **$4.23 million at Caesars**, about **$5 million at the Borgata** over five months, and **$5.8 million at the Tropicana** in one 12-hour session in April 2011, betting **$100,000 a hand**. New Jersey’s April 2011 filings show the Tropicana’s table-game win fell **54.4%** year-on-year, to $3.19 million, against a state-wide drop of 15.4%. He was not counting cards; the casinos were watching. He had negotiated a **trip-to-trip 20% rebate on losses once they passed $500,000**, plus rules (six-deck hand shuffle, dealer stands on soft 17, split and double on up to four hands) that by his figuring cut the house edge to **about 0.25%**. A rebate on losses is an option on the downside: in a near-coin-flip game where you stop at −$500,000 and pay $400,000, but keep playing while ahead, the expected value is positive — roughly **+$80,000 to +$90,000 per attempt** in the calculation below — even though the game itself is still against you. The Tropicana’s president, who had approved the $100,000 limit, was out within weeks, and every casino then pulled the terms.

## [Jontay Porter: the under only the player can guarantee, and the bets the NBA just removed](https://betkyo.com/en/blog/jontay-porter-the-under-only-the-player-can-guarantee-and-the-bets-the-nba-just-removed/)

_2026-10-19 · 8 min_

On **17 April 2024** the NBA banned **Jontay Porter**, a two-way player with the Toronto Raptors, for life. Its investigation found that before the **20 March 2024** game he told a known bettor about his health, then **played three minutes** and left saying he felt ill; an associate had placed an **$80,000 parlay** on Porter underperforming that **would have paid $1.1 million**, which the sportsbook **froze** as suspicious. Porter had also placed **at least 13 NBA bets through an associate’s account** between January and March 2024 — **$54,094 wagered, $76,059 paid, $21,965 net** — including parlays in which he bet on his own team to lose. In **July 2024** he pleaded guilty in federal court to **conspiracy to commit wire fraud**, saying he did it to get out from under gambling debts; four associates were charged. The arithmetic is why this bet type exists and why it is gone: a **player’s own “under” is the one market where one participant can make the outcome certain**, turning a parlay priced at roughly 13-to-1 into a near-sure thing. Sportsbooks dropped unders on two-way players in 2024, and in **October 2026** the NBA told its betting partners to remove **single-player under parlays**, **unders on players expected to play under 12 minutes**, **opening-tip winner bets** and **missed-shot bets** entirely.

## [Ippei Mizuhara: 19,000 bets, a 12% loss rate, and the bankroll that was never his](https://betkyo.com/en/blog/ippei-mizuhara-19000-bets-and-the-12-percent-that-broke-someone-elses-bankroll/)

_2026-10-18 · 8 min_

Between late 2021 and January 2024 **Ippei Mizuhara**, Shohei Ohtani’s interpreter, placed **about 19,000 sports bets** with an illegal bookmaker — roughly **25 a day, averaging about $12,800 each**. His winning bets came to **$142.3 million**, his losing bets to **$182.9 million**: a **net loss of about $40.7 million**, or **12.5% of everything he wagered**. To cover the debts he moved **about $17 million** out of Ohtani’s bank account, impersonating the player on calls to the bank. He pleaded guilty in 2024 to bank fraud and filing a false tax return and in **February 2025 was sentenced to 57 months** in federal prison, with **$16,975,010** in restitution to Ohtani and **$1,149,400** to the IRS. The arithmetic is the lesson: a standard sports book keeps about **4.5%** of a break-even bettor’s money; losing 12.5% means he was picking at roughly **46%** against the 52.4% a bettor needs to break even at −110, and a bookmaker extending credit let him keep going long past the point where any prepaid balance would have stopped him.

## [The Stake hack: $41 million in nine hours, and the vault that provably fair does not cover](https://betkyo.com/en/blog/the-stake-hack-41-million-in-nine-hours-and-the-vault-provably-fair-does-not-cover/)

_2026-10-17 · 8 min_

At **12:48 UTC on 4 September 2023** an attacker began moving funds out of **Stake.com**’s hot wallets: about **$3.9 million in USDT** first, then roughly **6,000 ETH**, then more over the following minutes. Blockchain analysts put the total at about **$41.35 million** — roughly **$15.7 million on Ethereum, $17.8 million on BNB Chain and $7.8 million on Polygon**. Bitcoin, Litecoin, XRP, EOS and TRON wallets were untouched; Stake said user funds were safe, paused deposits and withdrawals, and reported every service restored at **21:28 UTC**, under nine hours after the first transaction. Co-founder Ed Craven said private keys were not compromised and that the breach targeted a service the casino used to authorise transactions on those three chains. Days later the **FBI attributed the theft to the Lazarus Group**, the North Korean state unit it also blamed for more than $200 million of 2023 crypto thefts. Nothing about the games, their seeds or their odds was involved — which is exactly what the incident teaches: **provably fair proves the dice, not the vault**.

## [The statistician who read scratch tickets without scratching them: Mohan Srivastava and the singleton flaw](https://betkyo.com/en/blog/mohan-srivastava-the-statistician-who-read-scratch-tickets-without-scratching-them/)

_2026-10-13 · 8 min_

**Mohan Srivastava**, a geological statistician in Toronto, found in 2003 that the Ontario Lottery and Gaming Corporation’s tic-tac-toe scratch ticket could be read before it was scratched. Each ticket showed **eight tic-tac-toe boards, 72 visible numbers from 1 to 39**; most numbers repeated across the boards, but a few appeared exactly once. Those **singletons** were almost always the numbers hidden under the latex, and **three singletons in a row on one board marked a winner about 90% of the time**. He timed the method at about 45 seconds a ticket, estimated it at roughly $600 a day, judged that beneath his consulting rate, and reported it: he sorted 20 unscratched tickets into winners and losers, mailed them to the lottery, and the game was pulled from stores within days. The flaw was not bad luck but design: a lottery that fixes how many winners it prints cannot also make every ticket random, and the numbers on the face were generated by the same algorithm that placed the prizes. A scratch card whose outcome is one draw from a committed seed has nothing on its face to read.

## [Uston v. Resorts: the 1982 ruling that a casino cannot bar a card counter](https://betkyo.com/en/blog/uston-v-resorts-the-1982-ruling-that-a-casino-cannot-bar-a-card-counter/)

_2026-09-16 · 9 min_

In **Uston v. Resorts International Hotel, Inc.**, 89 N.J. 163, decided **5 May 1982**, the New Jersey Supreme Court held unanimously that an Atlantic City casino could not exclude **Kenneth Uston** for **card counting**. The Casino Control Act gave the state’s Casino Control Commission exclusive authority over the rules of every licensed game, and Uston had broken none of them; in the court’s words, the Commission “has promulgated the blackjack rules that give Uston a comparative advantage, and it has sole authority to change those rules.” The same man had already lost in Nevada, where federal courts in **1977** and **1978** found no legal duty on a private casino to let him play. New Jersey answered its own ruling within weeks: the Commission adopted regulations in **June and August 1982** letting casinos shuffle after any round, use more decks and run continuous-shuffling shoes, and in **1991** let them change table limits at any time. A counter could no longer be barred in New Jersey; the shoe he was counting could be shuffled the moment it turned in his favour. On this site there is no shoe to shuffle: every blackjack card is an independent draw of floor(u × 52) from a committed seed, so counting has nothing to count and the house needs no countermeasure.

## [Tommy Carmichael and the light wand: what a slot sensor could not see](https://betkyo.com/en/blog/tommy-carmichael-and-the-light-wand-what-a-slot-sensor-could-not-see/)

_2026-09-11 · 8 min_

**Tommy Glenn Carmichael** was a Tulsa television repairman who, from about 1980 until his arrest in 1996, cheated slot machines across Nevada and beyond with home-made devices. The first was the **top-bottom joint**, a metal rod and a wire that tripped an electromechanical machine into paying; it earned him a prison sentence in 1985. On release he built the **monkey paw**, a bent rod fed up the payout chute to trip the coin-counting switch, and then, for the optical machines that replaced it, the **light wand**: a small bulb on a battery, slipped into the payout slot, that **blinded the sensor counting coins** so the hopper kept paying with no idea it had paid. He was arrested in Las Vegas in 1996, pleaded guilty in 2001, and was later placed in Nevada’s **Black Book** of people banned from every casino in the state. Every one of his devices worked on the same weakness: the machine decided one thing and measured another, and the measurement could be fooled. On this site a payout is not counted out of a hopper. It is **stake × multiplier, computed in cents** from a result that was itself a function of a committed seed, and there is no sensor between the decision and the credit.

## [The Ritz laser scanner: £1.3 million in two nights, and why it was not a crime](https://betkyo.com/en/blog/the-ritz-laser-scanner-and-the-1-3-million-that-was-not-a-crime/)

_2026-09-11 · 8 min_

In **March 2004** two Serbian men and a Hungarian woman won about **£1.3 million** over two visits to the roulette tables of the **Ritz Club** in London. Police alleged they used a **laser scanner** concealed in a mobile phone, linked to a small computer, to time the ball and the wheel during the first seconds of a spin and predict the **sector** where the ball would land, placing late bets on that stretch of numbers. This is **sector targeting**: it works because a real ball on a real wheel obeys physics that can be measured in the few seconds before betting closes. The three were arrested, but in **December 2004** the police announced no charges would be brought under the Gaming Act 1845, the law then in force, because nothing had been done to the ball, the wheel or the dealer. They kept the winnings. The Gambling Act 2005 later defined cheating to include interference with the process of a game and made it a criminal offence. On this site a roulette pocket is the first eight bytes of a hash, floor(u × 37), with no ball to time and no sector to predict; the only thing you can observe before a spin is the commitment to the seed.

## [Wheel bias: the two men who beat roulette by writing numbers down, and why a court said it was legal](https://betkyo.com/en/blog/wheel-bias-jagger-and-garcia-pelayo/)

_2026-09-09 · 9 min_

**Wheel bias** is any physical imperfection that makes some roulette pockets land more often than 1 in 37. A pocket that comes up 1 in 30 instead of 1 in 37 turns a 35-to-1 bet from a 2.7% loss into a **20% edge**, and the only way to find one is to record thousands of spins. **Joseph Jagger**, a British engineer, hired clerks to log the wheels at Monte Carlo in the early 1870s, found one with nine favoured numbers, and won a reported two million francs before the casino swapped its wheels. **Gonzalo García-Pelayo**, a Spanish record producer, did the same with his family at Casino Gran Madrid in the early 1990s, won well over a million euros in today’s money, was barred and accused of cheating, and in **2004 Spain’s Supreme Court ruled his method legitimate**: he had exploited the casino’s failure to maintain its wheels, not tampered with them. The contrast with Phil Ivey, whose edge sorting an English court called a sting, is the whole law of advantage play in two cases. On this site there is no wheel to wear: the pocket is floor(u × 37) from the committed seed, and any spin can be recomputed.

## [Edge sorting: how Phil Ivey read the backs of the cards, and why two courts called it cheating](https://betkyo.com/en/blog/phil-ivey-edge-sorting/)

_2026-09-03 · 10 min_

**Edge sorting** exploits a manufacturing asymmetry on the back of a playing card: on some decks the pattern is cut fractionally off-centre, so a card turned 180° looks different along its edge. In August 2012 **Phil Ivey** and his partner **Cheung Yin Sun** persuaded a croupier at **Crockfords** in London to rotate high-value cards “for luck”, then had the same decks kept in play through an automatic shuffler that preserved orientation. Ivey could then tell a high card from a low one before it was dealt, and won **£7.7 million** at punto banco. Crockfords returned his stake and kept the winnings. The High Court (2014), the Court of Appeal (2016) and the **UK Supreme Court (25 October 2017)** all ruled for the casino: the conduct was cheating, whether or not Ivey believed it was honest. The Borgata in Atlantic City, where the pair had won about **$9.6 million** the same year, sued in 2014 and won an order for repayment in 2016; that case settled in 2020. On a game whose cards are derived from a committed seed pair, there is no back to read.

## [The superuser: when the house could see your cards](https://betkyo.com/en/blog/the-superuser-scandal/)

_2026-08-31 · 10 min_

Between 2003 and 2008, insiders at **Absolute Poker** and **UltimateBet** used “superuser” access to watch opponents’ hole cards in real time. Nothing was rigged — the decks were honest, the software dealt fairly — the cheats simply **knew what you held**. Absolute refunded **$1.6 million** and was fined **$500,000**; UltimateBet was fined **$1.5 million** and ordered to repay **$22 million**, with 1994 WSOP champion **Russ Hamilton** named the main beneficiary. The case defines the limit of every randomness proof: a verified deal says the cards were fair, not that they were secret.

## [The auditor was the attack: Ronald Harris and the slot machine he was paid to check](https://betkyo.com/en/blog/ronald-harris-slot-machine-scandal/)

_2026-08-28 · 9 min_

**Ronald Dale Harris** was a computer engineer at the Nevada Gaming Control Board whose job was detecting software built to cheat at slot machines. Between 1993 and 1995 he used that access to modify machines so they would pay out on a **specific sequence of coins**, and later wrote a program that predicted which numbers a keno game’s pseudorandom generator would produce. He and **Reid E. McNeal** were charged on **15 January 1995** after attempting to collect a **$100,000** keno jackpot at Bally’s Park Place in Atlantic City. The case is the clearest demonstration of why “a trusted expert inspected it” is a weaker guarantee than a proof anyone can check.

## [The night black came up twenty-six times: Monte Carlo, 1913](https://betkyo.com/en/blog/the-monte-carlo-black-streak/)

_2026-08-07 · 7 min_

At the Casino de Monte-Carlo in August 1913, a roulette wheel is said to have landed on black **twenty-six times in a row**. Players lost heavily not because the streak was rigged but because they kept **increasing their bets on red**, reasoning that red was overdue. It never was: each spin is independent, and red’s chance stayed a fraction under half throughout. The run is roughly a **1 in 137 million** event on a single wheel — and near-certain to appear somewhere, given how many wheels have spun since.

## [The students who beat the house: how the MIT Blackjack Team ran counting as a business](https://betkyo.com/en/blog/mit-blackjack-team/)

_2026-07-24, revised 2026-09-01 · 10 min_

The **MIT Blackjack Team** industrialized card counting: spotters flat-bet tables while tracking the count, then signaled a **big player** to swoop in only when the shoe favored the player. The edge was real but small — roughly **1–2% at good counts** — and the operation succeeded on discipline, bankroll management and investor capital, not genius plays. It worked because a dealt shoe has *memory*; against a fresh committed shuffle every round, counting has nothing to count.

## [The Run: how Archie Karas turned $50 into $40 million — and why he had to lose it](https://betkyo.com/en/blog/archie-karas-the-run/)

_2026-07-24 · 9 min_

**Archie Karas** arrived in Las Vegas in December 1992 with $50, borrowed a stake, and ran it — through pool, poker and then craps at the Horseshoe — into a reported **$40 million** by 1995. He then lost it all within months. The lesson is not about talent: with a negative edge and no stopping rule, the probability of eventual ruin is **1**. The Run was variance; the ruin was arithmetic.
